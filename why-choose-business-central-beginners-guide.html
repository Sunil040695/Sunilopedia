Why Choose Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central?

A beginner's guide to the platform, business value and learning journey.

Freshers (Graduates)ERP & Functional ConsultingSeptember 2026
By Sunil Badiginchala · Microsoft Certified Trainer & Business Central Functional Consultant

If you are new to ERP, you may have heard of Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central but still wonder: Why should I learn it? What does it actually do? And where could it fit in my career?

This guide is designed as a short prerequisite for my upcoming beginner webinar. It does not claim that Business Central is the only ERP worth learning. Instead, it explains what Business Central is, the business problems it supports, why process thinking matters, and what a beginner can explore next.

In one sentence: Business Central sits at the intersection of business processes, finance, operations, technology and consulting.

1. What Is Microsoft Dynamics 365 Business Central?

Microsoft describes Business Central as a business management and ERP application for small and midsize organisations. Its capabilities cover areas including finance, sales, purchasing, inventory, projects, warehouse management, manufacturing and services.

That matters because businesses do not operate in isolated screens. A customer order can connect to fulfilment, inventory, invoicing, receivables and financial reporting. Business Central is designed to support connected business processes.

Official reference: Microsoft Learn — Business functionality supported by Business Central.

3. The Biggest Learning Opportunity: Think in Processes

Business problem→Business process→Business Central→Transaction→Entries→Reporting

A beginner may ask, “Where is the Sales Order page?” A Functional Consultant starts one level higher:

  • What is the customer trying to accomplish?
  • What information is required?
  • How will the order be fulfilled?
  • How will it be invoiced?
  • What happens to inventory, receivables and the General Ledger?
The mindset shift: move from “Which screen do I use?” to “What business process am I supporting?”

4. What Can You Learn Through Business Central?

  • Finance — General ledger, receivables, payables, cash flow, fixed assets and financial analysis.
  • Sales — Customers, quotes, orders, shipments, invoices, returns and sales information.
  • Purchasing — Vendors, purchase orders, receipts, invoices and purchasing processes.
  • Inventory — Items, availability, movements, adjustments and costing.
  • Projects — Resources, budgets, progress, time and project-related costs.
  • Warehouse & Operations — Warehouse processes, planning, manufacturing, assembly and related operational flows.

5. Why Is Business Central Relevant to a Functional Consultant?

Business Central is not only about entering transactions. A Functional Consultant works where business requirements meet the ERP solution.

  • Understand business processes
  • Gather requirements
  • Translate requirements into Business Central functionality
  • Configure standard capabilities
  • Identify gaps and potential extensions or integrations
  • Support testing and user acceptance
  • Support data migration and training
  • Work with developers and technical consultants
A habit worth developing: ask “What business problem are we trying to solve?” before “Which field should I configure?”

7. Three Beginner Misconceptions to Avoid

“I need to know everything before I start.”

You do not. Start with ERP basics, one business process and core Business Central concepts.

“Business Central is only finance.”

Finance is important, but the platform also covers sales, purchasing, inventory, projects, warehouse, manufacturing, service and more.

“I only need to memorise the screens.”

Screens become easier when you understand the business process behind them.

8. Why the Learning Journey Is Still Evolving

Microsoft's current 2026 materials describe continued investment in AI-driven ERP capabilities, automation, analytics, supply chain management, security and platform improvements. For learners, the fundamentals remain important: business processes, master data, transactions, posting, configuration and reporting. New capabilities can then be understood in that context.

Learn the fundamentals first. Then learn how AI, automation, integrations and analytics extend those fundamentals.

9. A Simple Beginner Roadmap

01 · ERP FundamentalsUnderstand how organisations operate.
02 · Business ProcessesStart with Sales-to-Cash and Purchase-to-Pay.
03 · Business Central BasicsNavigation, customers, vendors, items, G/L accounts and documents.
04 · Functional DepthDimensions, posting, workflows, reporting and configuration.
05 · Implementation ThinkingRequirements, testing, migration and user acceptance.
06 · Practice & ShareBuild scenarios, explain them, create demos and document learning.

10. Is Business Central Worth Exploring for You?

Ask yourself:

  • Do I enjoy understanding how a business operates?
  • Do I like connecting information across departments?
  • Do I enjoy solving process problems?
  • Am I comfortable asking business users questions?
  • Do I want to combine business knowledge with technology?
  • Would I enjoy learning an ERP platform step by step?

You do not need every answer to be “yes.” The purpose is to understand whether this kind of work and learning interests you.

Final Thoughts

Choosing to learn Business Central does not mean deciding your entire career today. It means exploring whether you enjoy the combination of business processes, technology, problem solving and continuous learning that ERP work involves.

Start small: understand one business process, learn how Business Central supports it, practise it, and explain it to someone else.

Understand the business. Understand the process. Understand Business Central. Then keep practising.
